Installation-view conventions, and artists who push back on them

The problem

An "installation view" reads as neutral documentation — a record of what was in the room — but a photograph can only show one vantage at a time, and whoever chooses that vantage is making an editorial decision about what the work was. For an exhibition whose whole argument is inspectability and honest evidence rather than curated impression, it matters whether our own documentation photography is quietly doing the same shaping work it claims to avoid.

What they did

The essay "Blowing Up" argues that photographers who document art are unusually exposed to this problem, because art is already an exercise in subjectivity and the documentation photographer inherits real power over how a show is remembered. It cites artists' own installation photographs (Brancusi's, in particular) as reading almost like personal writing rather than neutral record, and profiles three Toronto-based artists whose work directly engages and complicates the convention of the standard installation shot, rather than treating it as a transparent window onto the work.

What we take

Not a technique to copy — a check to run on ourselves. Before we settle on a fixed documentation convention for the exhibition (angle, framing, what's in and out of shot for a rack or an installed piece), it's worth asking whether that convention is actually neutral or whether it's making an argument the way a curator's install itself makes an argument. The honest answer is probably that ours already does — choosing what counts as "legible" is itself a framing choice — which isn't a reason to abandon the rule, but a reason to be explicit in wall text that the documentation photography is also authored, not just recorded.

What doesn't transfer: the essay's subjects are working against the convention as an artistic move in its own right; our documentation photography has a job to do (comparability, evidence) that benefits from a consistent convention even if that convention isn't perfectly neutral.
