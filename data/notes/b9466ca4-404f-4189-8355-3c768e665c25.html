Three kinds of page, and one rule for choosing

The rule is about authorship. If the words are yours and the idea is yours, it's a note. If you're recording something someone else made, it's a source. If you're arranging other pages into an argument, it's a topic.

Keeping sources separate from notes is the load-bearing part. A source page is allowed to be thin — a link, a date, three sentences of why. A note is expected to make a claim you'd defend. Mixing them produces pages that do neither job, and you stop writing because every entry feels like it owes an essay.

One idea per note

If a note grows a second heading that could stand alone, split it and link the two. Splitting is cheap; untangling a 2,000-word note two years later is not.
